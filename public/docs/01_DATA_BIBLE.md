# HH Trucks - Data Bible

The Data Bible documents every business entity used by HH Trucks. It explains what each entity represents, why it exists, what information it stores, how it relates to other entities, and where it is used throughout the system.

Its purpose is to describe the business, not the implementation.

---

# Company

## Purpose

A Company represents any organization that HH Trucks conducts business with.

A company can act as:

- A customer who gives transport work.
- A transporter who performs transport work.
- The owner of one or more trucks.
- An internal company belonging to the HH Trucks group.

Every company is created only once and referenced throughout the system. Company information is never duplicated inside trips, payments or reports.

---

## Lifecycle

A company is normally created before operational work begins.

Once created it may be referenced by:

- Trips
- Trucks
- Customer Payments
- Transporter Payments

A company remains in the system even if it is no longer active, since historical trips and financial records continue to reference it.

---

## Fields

### id

Unique identifier generated by the system.

Used internally by the database and never shown to users.

---

### name

The legal or commonly used business name.

Examples:

- ABC Logistics
- XYZ Traders
- HH Trucks

This is the primary way users identify a company.

---

### isInternal

Determines whether the company belongs to HH Trucks.

Internal companies are used to distinguish owned fleet operations from third-party transport operations.

Values:

- true = Internal company
- false = External company

---

### createdAt

The date and time the company was added to the system.

Used mainly for auditing.

---

## Relationships

A Company may have:

- Many Customer Trips
- Many Transporter Trips
- Many Trucks

A Trip references a company instead of storing company information directly.

A Truck may optionally belong to a company.

---

## Used By

Company information is used in:

- Trip Creation
- Accounts
- Receivables
- Payables
- Customer Payment History
- Transporter Payment History
- Fleet Management
- Reports
- Dashboard

---

## Business Rules

A company should exist only once.

Multiple trips should reference the same company rather than creating duplicate records.

Changing a company's name automatically affects every trip and report that references it.

Deleting companies that are referenced by trips should not be allowed.

Internal companies represent businesses operated by HH Trucks.

External companies represent customers and transporters.

---

# Truck

## Purpose

A Truck represents a vehicle that can be assigned to transport work.

Every trip is performed using exactly one truck.

A truck may belong to an internal company or another company depending on operational ownership.

The truck acts as the central point for fleet management, maintenance history and operational reporting.

---

## Lifecycle

A truck is normally registered once.

After registration it can:

- Perform trips
- Accumulate maintenance history
- Record compliance expenses
- Record operational expenses

The truck remains in the system permanently so historical trip and maintenance information is preserved.

---

## Fields

### id

System generated unique identifier.

---

### numberPlate

Official vehicle registration number.

This uniquely identifies the truck.

Examples:

TN09AB1234

No two trucks may share the same registration number.

---

### companyId

Optional reference to the company that owns or operates the truck.

---

### registrationDate

Official vehicle registration date.

Used for fleet records.

---

### vehicleType

Classification of the vehicle.

Current values:

- Truck
- Tempo
- Car

This allows the system to support different vehicle categories in the future.

---

### createdAt

Date the truck was added to HH Trucks.

---

## Relationships

A Truck:

- Performs many Trips
- Has many Truck Expenses
- May belong to one Company

---

## Used By

Truck information is used by:

- Trip Creation
- Fleet Register
- Maintenance
- Truck Expenses
- Dashboard
- Accounts
- Profitability Reports
- Vehicle Compliance Tracking

---

## Business Rules

Every truck must have a unique number plate.

Trips cannot exist without a truck.

Truck maintenance belongs to the truck, not to individual trips.

Changing truck information should not affect historical maintenance records.

Compliance expenses such as insurance, permit and fitness belong to the truck rather than to any specific trip.

# Trip

## Purpose

A Trip represents a single transportation job.

It records the complete commercial and operational lifecycle of transporting goods from a source location to a destination.

Every financial transaction, operational expense, settlement, payment and profitability calculation ultimately belongs to a Trip.

The Trip is the central business entity in HH Trucks.

---

## Lifecycle

A trip progresses through three stages.

### Planned

A trip has been created but transportation has not yet started.

Information such as truck, customer, source and destination are known.

Financial values may still change.

---

### Active

The truck is currently performing the transport work.

Expenses and payments may continue to be recorded.

---

### Closed

The trip has been completed.

Final revenue, expenses and balances have been calculated.

Customer and transporter settlements should be complete.

Closed trips become historical business records.

---

## Ownership

The Operations department owns the Trip.

Accounts uses the financial information stored on the trip but does not own the operational record.

---

## Fields

### id

Unique system generated identifier.

Never shown to operators.

---

### truckId

References the truck performing the trip.

Every trip must belong to exactly one truck.

---

### source

Loading location.

Examples:

- Chennai
- Bangalore
- Salem

---

### destination

Delivery location.

Examples:

- Kochi
- Hyderabad
- Mumbai

---

### estimatedQty

Estimated quantity of goods.

Used mainly during planning.

Actual transported quantity may differ depending on business operations.

---

### ratePerUnit

Transport rate per unit.

Usually represents the agreed rate per tonne.

Primarily used when Revenue Mode is VARIABLE.

---

### revenueMode

Determines how customer revenue is calculated.

Current values:

- FIXED
- VARIABLE

Fixed revenue uses Gross Amount.

Variable revenue uses transported quantity and rate.

---

### loadType

Determates how the trip is categorized.

Current values:

- COMPANY
- EXTERNAL

This value influences settlement calculations and business rules.

---

### status

Current operational status.

Possible values:

- PLANNED
- ACTIVE
- CLOSED

---

### startDate

Date transport begins.

---

### endDate

Date transport ends.

---

### createdAt

Date the trip was created.

---

### closedAt

Date the trip was closed.

---

### closedBy

User responsible for closing the trip.

Used for auditing.

---

### billNumber

Invoice or billing reference issued to the customer.

---

### gcNumber

Goods Consignment Number.

Represents the transport document associated with the trip.

Operators commonly identify trips using the GC Number.

---

### grossAmount

Total amount billed to the customer before deductions.

Represents the primary customer revenue for the trip.

---

### charges

Additional customer charges.

Examples include miscellaneous operational charges agreed with the customer.

---

### customerAdvance

Advance amount received from the customer before settlement.

---

### customerDiesel

Diesel supplied or paid by the customer.

Recorded separately because it affects settlement calculations.

---

### damageAmount

Amount deducted because of damaged goods.

---

### damageNotes

Explanation of the recorded damage.

---

### tds

Tax Deducted at Source retained by the customer.

---

### gcBalance

Outstanding amount remaining from the customer after considering payments and deductions.

Used by the Accounts module.

---

### clientCompanyId

Customer responsible for the transport work.

References Company.

---

### transporterCompanyId

Transport company performing the work when outsourced.

References Company.

---

### transporterAdvance

Advance paid to the transporter.

---

### transporterCharges

Additional transporter related charges.

---

### transporterFreight

Freight agreed with the transporter.

---

### transporterPayable

Final amount payable to the transporter.

Calculated during settlement.

---

### commissionPerTonne

Commission charged per tonne when applicable.

Only used for applicable business scenarios.

---

### freightWeight

Weight used for freight calculations.

---

### mamool

Operational miscellaneous expense.

Defaults to zero.

For company loads this is generally not applicable.

---

### tripDistance

Distance travelled during the trip.

Used for reporting and future operational analysis.

---

### finalRevenue

Final revenue after calculations are completed.

Usually populated when the trip is closed.

---

### finalExpenses

Final operational cost of the trip.

Represents the total expense incurred.

---

### finalBalance

Final profit or balance after revenue and expenses are considered.

Stored as the completed financial outcome of the trip.

---

## Relationships

A Trip belongs to:

- One Truck
- One Customer Company (optional)
- One Transporter Company (optional)

A Trip has many:

- Expenses
- Customer Payments
- Transporter Payments

---

## Used By

The Trip entity is used by:

- Trip Register
- Dashboard
- Accounts
- Receivables
- Payables
- Settlement
- Customer Payments
- Transporter Payments
- Expense Tracking
- Reports
- Profitability Analysis

---

## Derived Values

The following values are calculated using Trip data.

- Outstanding Customer Balance
- Transporter Outstanding
- Total Customer Received
- Total Transporter Paid
- Total Trip Expenses
- Final Revenue
- Final Expenses
- Final Balance
- Earnings Per Day
- Truck Profitability

---

## Business Rules

Every trip must have exactly one truck.

Trips may exist without payments.

Trips may exist without expenses.

Customer payments always belong to one trip.

Transporter payments always belong to one trip.

Operational expenses always belong to one trip.

Truck maintenance never belongs to a trip.

Trips should not be deleted after financial activity has been recorded.

Closing a trip represents completion of operational and financial processing.

Trip history should remain permanently available for reporting and auditing.

# Expense

## Purpose

An Expense represents an operational cost directly incurred while executing a specific trip.

Unlike Truck Expenses, these expenses belong only to one trip and are included in calculating the trip's operational cost and profitability.

Examples include fuel purchased during the trip, toll charges, loading, unloading, police payments, driver payments and miscellaneous operational expenses.

---

## Lifecycle

Expenses are normally created while a trip is active but may also be added before a trip is closed.

Once recorded, an expense becomes part of the trip's financial history.

Deleting expenses after settlement should be avoided.

---

## Fields

### id

Unique system generated identifier.

---

### tripId

References the trip that incurred the expense.

Every expense belongs to exactly one trip.

---

### category

Classifies the operational expense.

Current categories are:

- Fuel
- Toll
- Police
- Loading
- Unloading
- Repair
- Driver Payment
- Other

---

### amount

The monetary value of the expense.

---

### expenseDate

Date on which the expense occurred.

---

### note

Optional description explaining the expense.

Examples:

- Diesel filled before Salem
- Toll correction
- Driver food advance

---

### billPath

Optional reference to an uploaded invoice or supporting document.

Not every expense requires a bill.

---

## Relationships

Every Expense belongs to one Trip.

---

## Used By

- Trip Settlement
- Profit Calculation
- Expense History
- Dashboard
- Reports

---

## Business Rules

Expenses always belong to a Trip.

Expenses are included when calculating final trip expenses.

Bills are optional because operational expenses such as police payments or driver advances may not have invoices.

---

# Truck Expense

## Purpose

Truck Expenses record costs related to owning and maintaining a vehicle rather than performing a specific trip.

These expenses are part of fleet management and are independent of trip profitability.

Examples include insurance renewals, tyres, permits, fitness certificates and servicing.

---

## Lifecycle

Truck Expenses may be recorded at any time.

Many are recurring expenses that occur annually or periodically.

The expense remains part of the truck's maintenance history permanently.

---

## Fields

### id

Unique identifier.

---

### truckId

References the truck.

Every Truck Expense belongs to one truck.

---

- Driver Payment

- Electrical

- Add Blue

Compliance

- Road Tax
- Permit
- National Permit
- Fitness

Other

- Other

---

### amount

Cost of the maintenance activity.

---

### vendor

Workshop, supplier or service provider.

---

### expenseDate

Date of the expense.

---

### month

Accounting month used for reporting.

---

### year

Accounting year used for reporting.

---

### createdAt

Record creation timestamp.

---

### documentPath

Optional uploaded invoice or supporting document.

---

### expiryDate

Used for compliance items.

Examples:

- Insurance expiry
- Permit expiry
- Fitness expiry

---

### note

Additional comments.

---

## Relationships

Each Truck Expense belongs to one Truck.

---

## Used By

- Fleet Register
- Maintenance History
- Compliance Tracking
- Dashboard
- Reports

---

## Business Rules

Truck Expenses never belong to a Trip.

Compliance records should preserve historical renewals rather than replacing older records.

Expiry dates are only applicable for compliance-related categories.

# Customer Payment

## Purpose

Represents money received from the customer for a specific trip.

A trip may have multiple customer payments.

Payments continue until the customer's outstanding balance becomes zero.

---

## Fields

### tripId

Trip receiving the payment.

---

### amount

Amount received.

---

### mode

Payment method.

Current values:

- Cash
- UPI
- Bank

---

### paymentDate

Date payment was received.

---

### type

Purpose of the payment.

Current values:

- Advance
- Settlement

---

### note

Optional remarks.

---

### createdAt

Timestamp when the payment was recorded.

---

## Relationships

Each payment belongs to one Trip.

---

## Used By

- Accounts Receivable
- Outstanding Calculations
- Customer Ledger
- Dashboard

---

## Business Rules

Customer payments reduce customer outstanding.

Multiple payments may exist for a single trip.

Payments are permanent financial records.

---

# Transporter Payment

## Purpose

Represents payments made to transporters performing transport work.

A trip may contain multiple transporter payments until settlement is complete.

---

## Fields

### tripId

Associated trip.

---

### amount

Amount paid.

---

### mode

Cash, UPI or Bank.

---

### paymentDate

Date payment was made.

---

### note

Optional remarks.

---

### createdAt

Timestamp of record creation.

---

## Relationships

Each Transporter Payment belongs to one Trip.

---

## Used By

- Accounts Payable
- Outstanding Calculations
- Transporter Ledger
- Dashboard

---

## Business Rules

Transporter payments reduce transporter payable amounts.

Multiple payments may exist for one trip.

# Operational Flow

A normal transport operation follows this sequence.

1. Create or select Companies.
2. Register or select a Truck.
3. Create a Trip.
4. Record operational Expenses during the trip.
5. Record Customer Payments as money is received.
6. Record Transporter Payments when money is paid.
7. Close the Trip after financial settlement.
8. Use the completed trip for reporting and business analysis.

# Data Ownership

Business information has a single owner.

Company owns company information.

Truck owns vehicle information.

Trip owns transport information.

Expense owns operational trip costs.

Truck Expense owns maintenance and compliance costs.

Customer Payment owns customer payment transactions.

Transporter Payment owns transporter payment transactions.

Import Session owns import metadata.

No business fact should have multiple owners.

---

# Guiding Principles

HH Trucks follows these principles.

- Every trip belongs to one truck.
- Operational expenses belong to trips.
- Maintenance expenses belong to trucks.
- Payments belong to trips.
- Reports never own business data.
- Business calculations should be derived wherever possible.
- Historical records should remain immutable once financial processing is complete.
- Every business record should have a single source of truth.
